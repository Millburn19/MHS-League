Records every way

vs median, vs average, EDWA, all-play, schedule swap

Each team's real record next to what it would be against the weekly median, against the weekly average, with a median game added, and using EDWA. Sorted by the with-median record.

How it's calculated

Vs median counts a win for every week a team scored above that week's league median (the middle of all 10 scores) and a loss below it. Vs average does the same against that week's league average (mean) score. EDWA replay replays the games already played using each team's current EDWA instead of its actual score, so the team with the higher EDWA wins each game. EDWA final is the real record plus the remaining games each team would win on EDWA. With median game adds that to the real record, as if every week also had a game against the median. All-play is the record from playing all 9 other teams every week, and all-play pace scales that to one game a week. Median vs actual is median wins minus real wins: positive means the real record is worse than the scoring deserved.

Power ranks

How it's calculated

Power score = 65% EDWA + 35% season average, in points per week. EDWA is an exponential-decay weighted average that counts the latest week fully and each earlier week at 0.8 times the week after it. All-play columns are shown for reference.

Playoff odds

How it's calculated

From 10,000 simulations of the rest of the season against the real schedule. Each team's expected weekly score is its season average pulled part of the way toward the league average, which matters most early in the season. Top 6 make the playoffs and the top 2 get a bye. Status counts wins only: clinched means not enough teams can reach the team's current win total to push it out, and eliminated means six teams already have more wins than it can reach. "Likely in at" is the worst final record that still makes the playoffs in at least 80% of simulations. Biggest game is the remaining game that changes the team's odds most between a win and a loss; swings are in percentage points of playoff odds.

Luck index

Actual wins minus the wins a team's scores deserved. Positive means winning more than the scores earned.

How it's calculated

Expected wins averages three ways of judging a week that ignore who you happened to play. All-play: the share of the other 9 teams you outscored (beating 6 of 9 counts as 0.67 of a win). Median: a win for every week above the league median. Average: a win for every week above the league's mean score, which one huge score can pull up.

Scoring profile

Recent weeks, averages, recent form and consistency. Shaded by rank in each column.

How it's calculated

EDWA is an exponential-decay weighted average: the latest week counts fully and each earlier week counts 0.8 times the week after it, so recent form matters most. SDFM (standard deviations from mean) is EDWA minus the season average, divided by the team's standard deviation: positive means trending up. Std dev is how much a team's score swings week to week.

Dispersion

box and whisker plot of every score

Every weekly score as a box and whisker plot, sorted by median. Hover a team for exact values.

How it's calculated

The box runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile, the line inside it is the median, the whiskers reach the ceiling (best week) and floor (worst week), and the dot is EDWA.

Depth and injury risk

what an injury would cost, bench value

How much each team loses when a starter goes down, and how much useful depth sits on the bench.

How it's calculated

Built from current rosters, valuing each player at his season points per game. Full strength is the best legal lineup from available players: anyone in an IR slot or listed as out or suspended is left out. If top starter is out is the weekly drop from losing the most valuable starter after the best bench player steps in. Avg loss per injury is that drop averaged over every starter. Bench VORP adds up how far each bench player sits above replacement level, which is the best player at his position who wouldn't start on any team in this league.

Injury impact

Teams ranked from most to least hurt by absences, weighted by how good the missing players are, not just how many games they missed.

How it's calculated

Each week, the page builds the best lineup a team could have fielded from everyone on its roster (players on bye left out), valuing each player at his points per game this season. It then builds the best lineup from only the players who actually played that week. The gap is the points of lineup strength lost to injuries and other absences, so losing a star costs far more than losing a backup who wouldn't have started. Season pts lost adds up every week. Weeks hit counts weeks with a gap of 5 or more. Losses it cost counts losses where the gap was bigger than the losing margin. Biggest absence is the lineup-worthy player whose missed games added up to the most points per game times weeks out; a strong bench can still cover for him, which is why the season total can be small. Lost now / wk is the same gap for the coming week, using ESPN's current injury designations (out, IR, suspended).
